How Neural Networks Discover Physical Laws from Data

A graduate-level walk through the ideas that turned deep learning from empirical alchemy into principled chemistry — from naive regression that violates physics, to architectures that discover the equations governing real-world systems. With runnable code in your browser.

§1The naive approach: black-box NN fits data, violates physics

Start with the obvious thing. Collect trajectory data from a double pendulum — positions, velocities, accelerations — and train a feed-forward neural network to map (q, q̇) directly to q̈. The network has millions of parameters, a smooth activation, and a standard MSE loss. Training proceeds as expected: the loss curve drops, the network fits every training point, and you declare victory.

Then you integrate forward. You take the network's predicted q̈, step the state forward with Euler, and watch what happens over a long horizon. The energy blows up. Not because the network is wrong on the training data — it isn't — but because nothing in the architecture or the loss told it that energy has to be conserved. The network learned a local approximation that, when composed with itself thousands of times, drifts unphysically. The same failure mode haunts standard numerical integrators like RK4 and ode45 on chaotic systems: they trade short-term accuracy for long-term structural violation.

Training loss (looks great)
MSE drops to ~0.001. By the standard ML metric, the model has converged.
Energy over time (the truth)
Total energy E(t) drifts to infinity. The model violates conservation by construction.
The naive loss

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{naive}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\mathbf{\ddot{q}}_{\text{obs},i} - f_\theta(\mathbf{q}_i, \mathbf{\dot{q}}_i)\right\|_2^2$$

The loss says nothing about physics. It only says "match these numbers." A network trained this way is a sophisticated interpolator with no structural awareness of conservation, symmetry, or variational principles. It is AI as alchemy: powerful, but without the constraints that make a model trustworthy outside its training distribution.

The question is not "can the network fit the data?" — it can. The question is "does the fit respect the physics?" — and without architectural intervention, it does not.

§2PINN: physics in the loss via autograd

The Physics-Informed Neural Network (Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis, 2019) takes a different stance. Instead of asking the network to predict accelerations directly, it asks the network to predict the solution field u(x, t) and then checks whether that field satisfies the governing PDE. The check is done by automatic differentiation: PyTorch or JAX computes the exact partial derivatives ∂u/∂t, ∂u/∂x, ∂²u/∂x² of the network output with respect to its inputs. No finite differences. No mesh. The PDE residual becomes a term in the loss function.

The PINN composite loss

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(\theta) = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}(\theta)}_{\text{fit sparse measurements}} + \lambda \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}(\theta)}_{\text{PDE residual at collocation points}}$$

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N_v}\sum_{i=1}^{N_v}\left\|\mathcal{N}_{\text{PDE}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x}_i, t_i; \theta)\right)\right\|_2^2$$

The key trick is the virtual collocation points. Real measurements are scarce — maybe N_d = 20 sensor readings. But the physics loss can be evaluated at N_v = 10,000 randomly sampled points in the spatiotemporal domain where no measurement exists. At each, the loss simply asks: does the PDE residual equal zero? Physics is abundant; data is scarce. The PINN offloads its data requirement onto the physics.

Sparse data (gold) + dense collocation (cyan) + PDE residual shrinking during training
As training progresses, the PDE residual (gold dots) shrinks toward zero across the entire domain — not just at the measurement points.

Soft means approximate

Physics enters as a penalty term, not a hard constraint. So min_θ (L_data + λ L_physics) yields a solution where L_physics(θ*) > 0 — the PDE is approximated, not exactly satisfied. This is the trade-off: PINNs are flexible and easy to train, but conservation laws hold only up to the residual. Later sections show how Hamiltonian and Lagrangian NNs enforce conservation by construction instead.

§4SINDy: discovering the actual equation

The code above demonstrates the core idea of Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (Brunton, Proctor, Kutz, 2016). The insight is deceptively simple: most physical equations are sparse in their term structure. Newton's second law F = ma has two terms. The Lorenz system has five. The Navier-Stokes equations have a handful. Even though the space of possible functions is infinite, the space of plausible physical laws is small.

SINDy operationalizes this by building a library Θ(X) of candidate terms — constants, linear terms, quadratic products, trigonometric functions — and then solving a sparse regression problem to find the smallest subset of terms that explains the observed derivatives.

The SINDy objective

$$\dot{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{\Theta}(\mathbf{X})\,\mathbf{\Xi}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{\Xi}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{\Xi}} \|\dot{\mathbf{X}} - \mathbf{\Theta}\mathbf{\Xi}\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{\Xi}\|_0$$

Candidate library (dense)
All candidate terms before thresholding. Most coefficients are small noise.
Discovered equation (sparse)
After STLSQ thresholding, only the true governing terms remain.

The λ∥Ξ∥₀ penalty (the L0 norm, counting non-zero entries) is NP-hard to optimize exactly. STLSQ approximates it by iteratively fitting, thresholding small coefficients to zero, and refitting on the remaining terms. The result is a human-readable equation that a physicist can inspect, verify, and reason about — not a black-box weight matrix.

Schmidt and Lipson (2009) showed that symbolic regression can rediscover Hamiltonians and conservation laws from raw trajectory data alone, with no prior knowledge of the physics. SINDy is the modern, efficient descendant of that idea.

§5HNN: conservation by construction

PINNs approximate physics; Hamiltonian Neural Networks (Greydanus, Dzamba, Yosinski, 2019) enforce it. Instead of predicting q̇ and q̈ directly, an HNN parameterizes a scalar Hamiltonian H_θ(q, p) with a neural network. The equations of motion fall out of Hamilton's canonical equations via autodiff:

Hamilton's canonical equations

$$\dot{q} = \frac{\partial H_\theta}{\partial p}, \quad \dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H_\theta}{\partial q}$$

The network learns the energy surface; physics generates the dynamics. And critically, Noether's theorem guarantees that because the Hamiltonian has no explicit time dependence, energy is conserved by construction:

Proof: dH/dt = 0 by anti-symmetry

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial q}\dot{q} + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\dot{p} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial q}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p} + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\left(-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}\right) = 0$$

The two terms cancel exactly. Conservation is not learned by accident — it is mathematically guaranteed by the anti-symmetric structure of Hamilton's equations. This is the deep insight: architecture as inductive bias. The HNN does not need to be told to conserve energy; the architecture makes violation impossible.

§6LNN: coordinates that match sensors

HNNs have one practical limitation: they require canonical momentum p. For a simple pendulum, p = m⋅l²⋅q̇ — easy to compute. For a multi-joint robotic arm, a fluid simulation, or a biological tissue, the canonical momentum is messy or impossible to derive from sensor data. Real sensors measure position q and velocity q̇, not p.

Lagrangian Neural Networks (Cranmer et al., 2020) remove this requirement. They parameterize a scalar Lagrangian L_θ(q, q̇) directly in the measurable coordinates and use the Euler-Lagrange equation to derive accelerations via autodiff:

Euler-Lagrange and the Hessian inversion

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \ddot{q} = \left(\nabla_{\dot{q}}\nabla_{\dot{q}}^T L\right)^{-1}\!\left(\nabla_q L - \left(\nabla_q\nabla_{\dot{q}}^T L\right)\dot{q}\right)$$

The velocity-Hessian ∇²_q̇ L plays the role of the mass matrix. Modern autodiff frameworks (JAX, PyTorch) compute these second derivatives in a few lines of code — a feat that was computationally prohibitive before 2018. The network learns L; the Euler-Lagrange operator produces q̈; the loss fits observed accelerations.

LNN: from scalar L through gradients and Hessians to acceleration
The scalar L (gold) feeds four gradient computations (cyan, pink, purple, green) that combine via Hessian inversion to yield q̈ (green circle).

Same conservation guarantee as HNN (energy is still a Noether invariant of the Lagrangian), but in the coordinates your sensors actually report. This is why LNNs are the practical choice for robotics, biomechanics, and any domain where canonical momentum is not directly measurable.

§7Neural ODE: continuous depth and the adjoint method

Step back and look at the architecture pattern across PINNs, HNNs, and LNNs: all of them use a neural network to define a vector field that is then integrated forward in time. Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt, and Duvenaud (2018) made this explicit with Neural Ordinary Differential Equations. The insight starts with ResNets.

A Residual Network block updates x_{k+1} = x_k + F(x_k, θ). This is exactly one step of forward Euler integration with step size Δt = 1 for the ODE dx/dt = F(x, t, θ). The layer index k is a discrete time step. Forward Euler is the simplest, least accurate ODE solver — prone to instability and cumulative error. The Neural ODE takes the limit Δt → 0 and replaces the discrete stack with a continuous vector field integrated by a high-order adaptive solver:

From ResNet to Neural ODE

$$\text{ResNet: } \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + F(\mathbf{x}_k, \theta) \quad \xrightarrow{\Delta t \to 0} \quad \text{Neural ODE: } \frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}(t), t)$$

ResNet: discrete Euler steps
Layer-by-layer updates with Δt = 1. Coarse, prone to drift.
Neural ODE: continuous trajectory
Integrated by adaptive DOPRI5. Smooth, accurate, mesh-independent.

The adjoint sensitivity method

Training a Neural ODE requires backpropagating through the ODE solver. Naively, this means storing every intermediate step of the forward integration — prohibitive for high-accuracy solvers. The adjoint method introduces a time-dependent Lagrange multiplier a(t) = -∂L/∂x(t) and integrates it backward in time alongside the reversed ODE:

Adjoint equation (O(1) memory)

$$\frac{da}{dt} = -a^T \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$$

The memory cost becomes constant in integration depth. A 10,000-step forward integration backprops with the same memory as a 10-step one. This is the Lagrange-multiplier trick of optimal control, applied to continuous-time deep learning. The same adjoint idea underlies the variational data assimilation methods (4D-VAR) used in numerical weather prediction — Neural ODEs just made it standard in ML toolkits.

And the payoff for physics-informed ML: replacing the generic RK45 solver with a symplectic or variational integrator turns a Neural ODE into a Hamiltonian or Lagrangian Neural Network. One framework, many physics constraints.

§8Synthesis: where these ideas meet the real world

The architectures above are not academic curiosities. They are deployed today in systems where physical correctness is not optional — where a model that violates conservation can cause a robot to jerk dangerously, a fusion reactor to lose plasma containment, or a surgical plan to misestimate blood flow. Here are four concrete deployments, each tied to the concept that enables it.

🧮
Prosthetic joints
LNN · 10× less data
Smooth energy-efficient joint trajectories from position/velocity sensors. No canonical momentum needed — critical for multi-joint limbs.
⚡
Fusion plasma
PINN · sensors can't survive
Infer internal tokamak plasma profiles from sparse outer-wall measurements + MHD physics loss. Stable plasma control for carbon-free energy.
⚙
Cardiovascular flow
FNO · 23,000× faster than CFD
Patient-specific Navier-Stokes super-resolution from MRI. Surgeons test interventions in real time before cutting.
🚀
Orbit tracking
HNN · decades-long stability
Energy-conserving satellite and debris tracking over years. No false collision warnings from energy drift.

The pattern: each real-world problem has a physical structure that the right architecture can exploit. PINNs for systems where the PDE is known but the solution is hard. SINDy for systems where the equation itself is unknown. HNNs/LNNs for systems where conservation is non-negotiable. Neural ODEs for systems where the dynamics are continuous and irregularly sampled. FNOs for systems where mesh-independent PDE solving at scale matters. The unifying principle across all of them: bake the physics into the architecture and the loss, and the network cannot help but respect the underlying laws.

This is the transition from alchemy to chemistry. Not smarter networks — structurally constrained ones. The physics is free; you just have to encode it.
