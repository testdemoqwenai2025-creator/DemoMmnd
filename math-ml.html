The Geometry of Attention

Linear algebra · How transformers see — the geometric structure of scaled dot-product attention
Scene 1/6
Token embeddings
🔍1.0x

    Deeper thoughts

    Attention is geometry. The scaled dot-product attention $A = \text{softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})V$ is fundamentally a geometric operation: queries and keys are directions in $\mathbb{R}^{d_k}$, and their dot product measures alignment. The softmax converts alignment scores into a probability distribution over which values to attend to.

    The $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling matters. Without it, dot products grow with dimensionality, pushing softmax into saturated regions where gradients vanish. This single scaling factor is what lets attention work at $d_k=64$ or $d_k=4096$.

    Multi-head = parallel geometric views. Each head projects Q, K, V into a different subspace, attends independently, and concatenates. This is like viewing the same scene through different lenses — one head might track syntax, another semantics.

    The Calculus of Learning

    Calculus · How machines learn — gradient descent on loss landscapes, from Newton to Adam
    Scene 1/6
    The loss landscape
    🔍1.0x

      Deeper thoughts

      Learning is calculus. Every neural network training step is the chain rule: $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$. The gradient $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ is computed by backpropagation — literally the chain rule applied layer by layer. Calculus is not adjacent to ML; it is ML.

      The loss landscape is not random. For over-parameterized networks, almost all local minima are global (good), but saddle points proliferate (bad). Momentum and adaptive methods (Adam) navigate saddle points by accumulating gradient direction across steps.

      The Schrödinger connection. Hamiltonian dynamics $\frac{d\psi}{dt} = -iH\psi$ and gradient descent $\frac{d\theta}{dt} = -\nabla L$ share the same mathematical structure: both evolve a state along the negative gradient of an energy function. Quantum mechanics and ML optimization are cousins.

      The Probability of Truth

      Probability & statistics · How machines reason — Bayesian inference, entropy, and the limits of compression
      Scene 1/6
      Prior beliefs
      🔍1.0x

        Deeper thoughts

        Bayes' theorem is the calculus of belief. $P(H|D) = \frac{P(D|H)P(H)}{P(D)}$ — the posterior is the prior updated by evidence. Every ML model that outputs a probability is implicitly Bayesian: the training data is evidence, the parameters are hypotheses, and the learned distribution is the posterior.

        Entropy connects probability to physics. The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy $S = \frac{A}{4L_P^2}$ and the Shannon entropy $H = -\sum p \log p$ are the same quantity. The information bottleneck principle — compress X while preserving Y — is the variational principle of statistical learning.

        Kolmogorov complexity is the ultimate prior. AIXI weights programs by $2^{-|p|}$ (Solomonoff induction) — the shortest program that explains the data is most likely. This is uncomputable but approximable. Every neural network is a computable approximation of this universal prior.
