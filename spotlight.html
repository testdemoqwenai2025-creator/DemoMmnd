im2sim: from 3D MRI to blood flow in 0.5 seconds

An open-source Python library (PyTorch + PyTorch Geometric + VTK + PyVista) that builds hybrid image-graph neural networks to predict time-varying pressure and velocity fields directly from 3D medical images. Six interactive clips walk through the architecture, the clinical impact, and the better approach we'd suggest. Each clip uses the same framework as Physics+ML — canvas animation, six playback controls, zoom, parameter slider, narration, and deeper thoughts.

Stack: PyTorch · PyG · VTK · PyVista Inference: <0.5s (vs hours CFD) Use case: pulmonary artery hemodynamics By: Anirudh Raman et al.

              Analysis: the better approach, summarized

              im2sim is excellent engineering. The 0.5s inference is clinically transformative. But four architectural additions would move it from "fast" to "trustworthy." Each connects to a concept in our Physics+ML curriculum.

              1. FNO-style spectral layer for the field head

              Replace the pixel-wise 3D CNN output with a Fourier Neural Operator spectral layer. Mesh-independent, captures global flow structure, and supports zero-shot super-resolution: train on 64² voxels, evaluate on 256². The vessel geometry doesn't need to be re-meshed for different resolutions.

              2. Physics-informed loss (divergence-free constraint)

              Add ∇·u = 0 as a soft penalty at collocation points inside the predicted field — exactly the PINN pattern. Training-only cost; structural mass conservation at inference. Without this, the network can produce physically impossible velocity fields that look plausible.

              3. Ensemble or Bayesian uncertainty

              The surgeon sees u(x,t) ± σ(x,t), not just a point estimate. Train an ensemble of 5-10 models with different initializations; the variance across the ensemble is the uncertainty estimate. Low-confidence regions (unusual anatomy, out-of-distribution) trigger a flag.

              4. Active learning loop

              Flagged low-confidence cases are queued for traditional CFD verification. The CFD results are added back to the training set. The network improves on exactly the cases it currently fails — closing the loop between fast inference and slow verification. This is the discrepancy modeling pattern applied to the training pipeline.

              The 0.5s number gets im2sim through the clinic door. Physics-informed loss, uncertainty, and active learning are what keep it in the clinic.
