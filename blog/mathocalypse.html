The Mathocalypse: What Happens When AI Solves the Unsolvable

Filed under: AI cognition, Erdős, UGC, Unitary Synthesis, Aaronson

Three named breakthroughs in 2024-2025 — Erdős disproved, UGC Lean-verified, Unitary Synthesis refuted — mark the crossing-point where AI stopped being a mathematical assistant and became the most powerful theorem prover on Earth. Scott Aaronson calls it the Mathocalypse. This post examines what that means for the practice of mathematics.

The Erdős unit distance conjecture, posed in 1946, asked whether the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among n points in the plane is achieved by a regular square grid. For 80 years, combinatorial geometers tried and failed to prove it. In 2026, an OpenAI model disproved it by constructing configurations that yield strictly more unit-distance pairs than the grid — using techniques from algebraic number theory, a domain that combinatorial geometers rarely visit.

The Unique Games Conjecture (Khot, 2001) asserts the NP-hardness of approximating certain constraint satisfaction problems. AI produced Lean-certified proofs incorporating novel error-correcting codes. If P ≠ NP, this pins SDP relaxation as the optimal polynomial-time approximation for a broad class of optimization problems.

Aaronson and Kuperberg's 2007 Unitary Synthesis Problem conjectured that arbitrary n-qubit unitaries require Ω(2^n) oracle queries. AI disproved this by construction — an oracle function exists allowing polynomial-time quantum state synthesis.

The advantage is not intelligence in the human sense. It is the absence of psychological cost. A human mathematician cannot try construction #847 after constructions #1-846 have failed. An AI can. This is the operational substrate of the Mathocalypse.
