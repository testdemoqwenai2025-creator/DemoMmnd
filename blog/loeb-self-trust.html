Löb's Theorem: The Self-Trust Trap in AI Alignment

Filed under: Philosophy, Löb, Gödel, alignment, AIXI

If a formal system proves that its own proofs imply truth, then by Löb's theorem it proves everything. The axiom schema is: □(□P → P) → □P. This is the self-referential core of provability logic.

The consequence for AI alignment is startling: an AI that trusts its own proof checker (believes □P → P for its own proofs) will, by Löb, prove anything it can state. This is the formal version of the "self-trust trap" that alignment researchers worry about.

Aaronson spent two years at OpenAI working on alignment. The Löb-style concern is not abstract: if an AI system can verify its own reasoning, and it believes its verification implies correctness, then the system has no epistemic constraints. It can "prove" anything.

The resolution is not to prevent self-trust, but to ensure that the AI's proof system is not closed under Löb's axiom. This requires either: (a) restricting the proof system's expressiveness, or (b) introducing an external verifier that the AI cannot fully model.

This connects to AIXI (Hutter, 2005): the theoretically optimal universal AI. AIXI is uncomputable (requires solving the halting problem), but it defines the formal ceiling on AI performance. The observatory holds AIXI and Löb as companion expressions: AIXI defines what's optimal; Löb defines what's dangerous.
