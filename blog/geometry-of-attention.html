The Geometry of Attention

November 4, 2026 · 8 min read · Part 1 of 3 in the Mathematics with ML series

When Vaswani et al. published "Attention Is All You Need" in 2017, they didn't just introduce a new architecture — they revealed that the core operation of modern AI is fundamentally geometric. The scaled dot-product attention is a geometric operation disguised as a formula.

The Formula

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

This looks like matrix multiplication, and it is. But let's unpack what each piece means geometrically.

Queries, Keys, and Values

Each input token is embedded as a vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$. Three learned projection matrices — $W_Q$, $W_K$, $W_V$ — transform each embedding into three different roles:

Query ($Q = X W_Q$): "What am I looking for?"
Key ($K = X W_K$): "What do I contain?"
Value ($V = X W_V$): "What information do I carry?"

These are not given — they are learned during training. The network discovers what to look for, what to offer, and what to transmit, all through gradient descent on the projection matrices.

The Dot Product Is Cosine Similarity

The core computation is $QK^T$ — the dot product of every query with every key. Geometrically, the dot product of two vectors is:

$$\vec{q} \cdot \vec{k} = |\vec{q}||\vec{k}|\cos\theta$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between them. After normalization, this is cosine similarity — a measure of how much two vectors point in the same direction. High alignment means high relevance. This is the heart of attention: geometry becomes semantics.

Why $\sqrt{d_k}$?

The scaling by $\sqrt{d_k}$ is not arbitrary. As dimensionality grows, dot products tend to grow in magnitude (the sum of $d_k$ terms). Without scaling, softmax would saturate — pushing most weights to 0 or 1, where gradients vanish. The $\sqrt{d_k}$ factor keeps the variance stable regardless of dimension:

$$\text{Var}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \approx 1 \quad \text{for all } d_k$$

Softmax: From Scores to Probabilities

Softmax converts raw alignment scores into a probability distribution:

$$\text{softmax}(x_i) = \frac{e^{x_i}}{\sum_j e^{x_j}}$$

Every token now has a probability distribution over all other tokens. The temperature parameter $\tau$ controls sharpness: $\text{softmax}(x/\tau)$. Low $\tau$ produces sharp attention (one token dominates); high $\tau$ produces uniform attention. The Weighted Sum

The output for each token is a weighted combination of all values:

$$\text{output}_i = \sum_j \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i K_j^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V_j$$

Tokens that attend strongly to each other blend their information. This is how context flows through a transformer — every token learns from every other, in a single matrix multiplication.

Multi-Head: Parallel Geometric Views

Multiple heads run in parallel, each projecting Q, K, V into a different subspace:

$$\text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \ldots, \text{head}_h)W^O$$

One head might track syntax, another semantics, another coreference. The outputs are concatenated and projected back. This parallel geometry is what gives transformers their power — they see the same input through multiple geometric lenses simultaneously.
