Kolmogorov Complexity and the Structure of Mathematical Creativity

Filed under: AI cognition, Kolmogorov, attention, creativity

When AI finds connections no specialist would have made, what exactly is it doing? The formal answer: discovering low-Kolmogorov-complexity bridges between distant concepts.

Kolmogorov complexity K(x) is the length of the shortest program that produces x on a universal Turing machine. It's well-defined but uncomputable (a variant of the halting problem). Despite being uncomputable, it provides the right intuition: short bridges between distant domains are rare and valuable.

The Erdős disproof used algebraic number theory — a high-K domain for combinatorial geometers, but a low-K bridge for the AI once the search was parallelized. The AI didn't need to "understand" algebraic number theory; it needed to find a construction whose description was short enough to fit in its search budget.

This connects to the attention mechanism: attention is essentially a learned Kolmogorov-complexity estimator. It learns which connections are "short" (high attention weight) and which are "long" (low attention weight). The transformer architecture succeeds because it implements a computable approximation of Kolmogorov complexity estimation.
